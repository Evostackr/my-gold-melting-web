Jewellery Costing Mathematical Formulations

Jewellery estimation must accurately account for stone weight deductions and processing losses to protect both the consumer and the jeweler. Here is the formal mathematics:

Net Gold Subject to Melting
Net Weight = Gross Gold Weight - AD Stone Weight + Total Casting Expenses
Pure Fine Gold Output
Fine Gold = (Net Weight × Melting Touch) / 100
Final Jewellery Costing Percentage
Jewellery Costing = (Net Weight × Melting Touch) / Gross Gold Weight

The 10 Manufacturing & Casting Loss Factors

In modern jewelry casting (centrifugal and vacuum induction), each step incurs a distinct loss. My Gold Melting incorporates all 10 loss calculators:

Loss Category Description Mathematical Formulation
1. Alloy Loss Loss incurred when blending master alloys into molten gold. (Alloy Wt × Alloy Rate) / ((Gold Rate × Melting) / 100)
2. Casting Loss Vaporization, crucible residue, and investment reaction. Direct gram equivalent loss
3. Failing Loss Filing, sprue cutoff, and coarse shaping metal filings. Direct gram equivalent loss
4. Sitting Loss Prong bending, stone collet adjustment, and seat cutting. Direct gram equivalent loss
5. Polishing Loss High-speed rouge, tripoli, and buffing wheel abrasion. Direct gram equivalent loss
6. Stone Bill Loss Monetary conversion of diamond/gemstone invoice to gold equivalent. Stone Bill Rs / ((Gold Rate × Melting) / 100)
7. Gillet Loss Electroplating, pickling, and flash coating losses. (Gold Wt × Gillet Rate) / ((Gold Rate × Melting) / 100)
8. Rhodium Loss White gold rhodium plating per piece or pair. (Pieces × Rhodium Rate) / ((Gold Rate × Melting) / 100)
9. Lathiya Pech Loss Threading, screws, safety catches, and findings loss. Direct gram equivalent loss
10. Extra Loss Ultrasonic cleaning, acid boil, and unforeseen workshop losses. Direct gram equivalent loss

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is AD Stone deducted from the gold weight?
American Diamonds (cubic zirconia) and precious stones have significantly lower monetary value than gold per gram. To calculate the genuine precious metal content, gemstone weight is subtracted from the gross weight before applying the melting touch.
What does the Jewellery Costing percentage represent?
The Jewellery Costing percentage represents your effective fine gold billing touch. For instance, an 87.16% costing means that for every 100g of gross jewelry produced, the client is charged 87.16g of pure fine gold to cover the metal, stone setting, and manufacturing losses.
Can I save and export these calculations?
Yes! In the full PWA web app, every jewellery costing calculation is saved locally to IndexedDB with a complete 14-field expense breakdown that you can review, inspect, or delete anytime.
