Goldsmith & Jeweller Calculation FAQ

Comprehensive, authoritative answers to everyday questions regarding gold & silver melting, purity touches, Palo additions, fine gold estimation, and casting loss accounting.

1. Gold Melting & Touch Purity Calculations

What is Touch (Touche) in gold and silver purity?
Touch represents the percentage purity of precious metal (gold or silver) on a scale of 0 to 100. For instance, standard 22 Karat jewelry has a touch of 91.6% (Hallmark 916), while 18 Karat gold has a touch of 75.0% (Hallmark 750).
How do you calculate weighted average touch when mixing different gold batches?
Multiply each sample weight by its touch percentage divided by 100 to get individual fine gold weights. Sum all fine gold weights and divide by the total combined weight, then multiply by 100:
Average Touch = (Σ (Weight_i × Touch_i) / Total Weight)
Can this tool handle decimal and fractional grams?
Yes. All calculations support unlimited floating-point precision and format results to three exact decimal places (0.000g), matching the standards of precision bullion analytical balances.

2. Palo (Alloy) vs. Fine Gold Addition

What is Palo in Indian gold melting?
Palo (पालो) refers to base alloy metal (such as copper, fine silver, zinc, or brass) added into high-purity melted gold to lower its purity down to the target touch (such as 91.6 or 75.0).
What is the difference between Add Palo and Add Fine Gold?
If the current metal batch has an average touch higher than your target touch, you must Add Palo (alloy) to dilute it. If the current batch has an average touch lower than your target touch, you must Add Fine Gold (100% pure gold) to elevate its purity.
What happens if my target touch equals 100%?
If the target touch is 100% (pure gold), no alloy or dilution is possible. The application alerts you with "Nothing to Add" because pure gold cannot be diluted while maintaining 100% purity.

3. Jewellery Costing & 10 Casting Expenses

Why is gemstone / AD Stone weight deducted in jewellery costing?
Gemstones like American Diamonds (cubic zirconia) are non-gold weight. Jewellers deduct this weight from the gross ornament weight so the customer is only charged the precious metal melting touch on actual gold.
What are casting losses and why are they added to jewellery costing?
During investment casting, microscopic gold is lost to vaporization, crucible slag, filing, and buffing. Converting these losses into equivalent gold weight and factoring them into jewellery costing ensures the goldsmith covers manufacturing costs without financial shortfall.
What are the 10 casting expenses tracked?
The 10 tracked expenses are: Alloy Loss, Casting Loss, Failing Loss, Sitting Loss, Polishing Loss, Stone Bill Loss, Gillet Loss, Rhodium Loss, Lathiya Pech Loss, and Extra Expenses.

4. Privacy, Offline & PWA Security

Can My Gold Melting be used offline without an internet connection?
Yes. My Gold Melting is built as an offline-first Progressive Web Application (PWA) with client-side caching. All calculations and history persistence run locally using browser memory and IndexedDB without transmitting data to remote servers.
Where is my calculation history stored?
All calculation history records are stored exclusively inside your browser's local IndexedDB database. You can inspect, review, or permanently delete any record at any time.
Does the application collect any personal or financial data?
No. We enforce a strict zero-telemetry policy. We do not track, collect, store, or transmit your calculation weights, touch figures, rates, or business transactions.
